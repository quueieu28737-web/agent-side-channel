\paragraph{Decision and evidence}
WRA provides a privacy-oriented reason to execute cover: generate plausible
alternative research activity around a user's real request. The authors issue
real and decoy prompts in parallel, return only the real output, and evaluate
interleaved visited-domain sets. With one decoy, the functional-intent
reconstruction metric falls from 0.770 to 0.610; this is positive evidence for
reducing the tested observer's prompt inference \cite{jeong2026wra}. The
report-usefulness scores in its separate visibility experiment do not establish
utility for that decoy condition.

\paragraph{Follow cover to execution}
We distinguish four deployment categories: transport dummies discarded before
dispatch; requests confined to an isolated shadow environment; read-only or
idempotent operations; and state-changing actions. These categories organize
the comparison rather than denote four new experiments. In particular,
idempotence concerns repetition and does not make an initial state change safe.
AHB illustrates why this distinction matters: Trip Planning success is 0.75
for Raw and 0.15 for combined online humanization, with unintended GUI
transitions discussed by the authors \cite{zhu2026turing}. This is a combined
configuration comparison, not an isolated Fake effect or a privacy estimate.

\paragraph{Permitted use and next decision}
Retain WRA's privacy result for its tested domain view and decoy construction.
When cover is demonstrably discarded or isolated, the direct state-changing
action mechanism is absent. When it reaches a tool or interface, establish
goal and policy compatibility for that action, including its initial effect.
WRA's research-task decoys motivate such execution; AHB identifies the state
risk that must be checked. Same-condition terminal utility, latency and wire
cost are still needed to choose an operating budget, rather than to erase the
already measured privacy benefit.

The primary V-minus-T comparison tests the dependency-oriented workflow against
an active, content-matched structured baseline, not against an artificially
incapable reader. V-minus-R is secondary. T is not prohibited from independently
discovering the same dependency organization. Thus the hypothesis can fail.

The conceptual difference is not exclusive access to graph reasoning: a
complete checklist already contains all substantive criteria. What changes is
the default order/index used to apply them under finite resource constraints.
This can yield usability/reliability differences in principle; it cannot yield
an unconditional logical or informational superiority theorem over an
unrestricted correct checklist implementation.

### Synthetic illustration, not a source-paper error

Two states emit [1,2] and [2,1]. A local count transform emits [2] in both states.
An additional permitted branch exports the original order after that transform.
A final receiver seeing both branches distinguishes the states. If that branch
is denied, the finite pair may instead have equal final views. If its permission
is unreported, the answer can depend on the supplied admissible permissions.
V recommends starting with the final view and tracing both branches. T can
arrive at the same correct result through its reconciliation stage. The oracle
tests exact projections, not whether a respondent uses the words SCOPE or join.

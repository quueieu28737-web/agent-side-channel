\section{Result-Pair and Six-Check Audit}\label{app:result-audit}
This retrospective audit applies the paper's comparison rules to the existing 33 result records across 22 families. It adds explicit source judgments and deterministic aggregation, not new measurements or independent human coding. The machine-readable inventory retains every unordered pair ($\binom{33}{2}=528$), the two proposed transfer directions, the coded reason and both source locators. Missing transfer evidence remains unresolved; known endpoint or mechanism conflicts are distinguished from missingness.

\subsection{Relevance and inference definitions}
Six mechanism-question groups define 102 related pairs: traffic-envelope mitigation (66), cache-service mitigation (28), behavioral humanization (3), generator fingerprint variation (3), local-memory mitigation (1), and fixed guardrail/instruction variation (1). Access control, contention noise and filter-created observations remain singleton contextual groups. The other 426 pairs are outside these specified questions, rather than silently excluded as failures. These are retrospective relevance bins, not a sample of all possible comparisons or a literature-wide denominator.

A descriptive comparison is licensed when the target and receiver-facing outcome align while each metric, population and execution mode remains explicit. It licenses discussion, not numerical ranking. A paired effect additionally requires common defended test units and a verified fitting/matching relation. Directional transfer asks whether a source operating point can be reused unchanged at the destination condition. An absent bridge is unresolved; a demonstrated wrong object or conflicting operating point blocks that particular reuse. Pairs can therefore receive different outcomes for the three inferences.

The 13 same-family pairs provide the least arbitrary comparison subset (Table~\ref{tab:pair-descriptive}). Seven compare aligned outcomes and six substitute different primitives or cost objects. Of the seven aligned-outcome pairs, four have known differences in collection/execution populations and three lack a verified paired-test/fitting binding. None licenses subtraction across those two records. This is separate from the positive paired adaptation contrasts already recorded \emph{inside} UI-delay-offline and DANGOL-capture. For unchanged deployment transfer, five same-family pairs have a positively conflicting requested reuse (10 directions) and eight lack a bridge (16 directions). Across the 102 related pairs, the 204 directions comprise 10 blocked and 194 unresolved cases. No cross-record positive bridge is established by this inventory; source-local permissions remain usable.

For example, transformed Dangol captures and its live loopback stack report different operating points (approximately 0.17/16.2$\times$ bytes versus 0.30/13$\times$ bytes/11$\times$ latency). They can be described together as workflow-recovery evidence but not as a paired deployment improvement. SpliceDefense's CTBF signal suppression and QCP-only cost likewise answer different component questions. The counts expose the consequences of the declared comparison, not an observed rate of community misreading or a benchmark of the field schema.

The joint deployment item is partial for all 16 because the recorded component, replay or separately collected results do not jointly bind every required deployment object. This does not impose an agent-level experiment on studies of individual components. AHB binds detectability and objective task success but not execution cost; Dangol's live stack binds inference and protocol costs but not objective task utility; Speculation reports a padding score and token-payload ratio. In the adaptation item, partial status also reflects unbound observer access or fitted-test details even when source-local protected fitting is established.

The audit corrects four historical interpretations through an additive overlay. Speculation uses a Random Forest, and Time Will Tell fits input-length-conditioned thresholds; their defended-fit/run bindings are \NRV{}, not inapplicable merely because the attack was described as matching or a count rule \cite{wei2024speculation,timewilltell2024}. SafeKV Figure~12 explicitly uses a five-second post-trigger window, resolving that field. SafeKV also reports separate serving-performance experiments: in the stated multi-turn comparison against full isolation, Qwen's extra TTFT falls from 50.41\% to 11.74\%, and DeepSeek's from 118\% to 34.28\%; at 16 requests/s its tested configurations achieve 1.36--2.66$\times$ the token throughput of Cache-Partition \cite{chu2025safekv}. These are service measurements, not only detector latency. They retain their own workload/condition rather than becoming a joint leakage--cost point with Figure~12. Original frozen records remain available alongside the corrected overlay.

\subsection{Reproduction and interpretation}
The package's \texttt{decision-audit/} directory contains the rules, explicit semantic coding, all-pair inventory, 1,056 directional rows, 96 checklist cells, source corrections and read-only checker. The checker verifies unique coverage, input hashes, source/result ownership and reproducibility of the saved counts without fitting or network access. Original full texts are not redistributed; URLs, versions, locators and hashes permit source lookup. Counts are descriptive: no significance test, pooled efficacy score, causal effect, reviewer-error rate or independent human agreement is estimated. Thus the audit quantifies what the source bindings permit while keeping the underlying evidence and comparison question visible.

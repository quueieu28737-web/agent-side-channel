\subsection{Boundary checks and extraction}
The fixed 24-known-paper set has 17 query recoveries (nine Semantic Scholar, fourteen arXiv, with overlap); the original 60-family set has 25 (fourteen and nineteen respectively). These checks show the additional route's concrete contribution. They do not establish calibrated database recall: index eligibility for each non-return has not been authenticated.

The exclusion check uses seed 20261021: all 177 exclusions matching the recorded high-risk title/abstract pattern, plus 27 other reason-stratified samples, capped at ten per reason. Four of 204 rows were rerouted. MIRAGE becomes one new diagnostic core; InputSnatch resolves to an already included title/version alias; two become adjacent contexts. The final disposition table incorporates these corrections. Because the check is enriched and uses the same AI-assisted workflow, four of 204 is an observed correction count, not a population miss-rate or independent-human agreement estimate.

Every new core row records observer, signal, target/protection relation, evaluation conditions, intervention, utility, cost, RQ contribution, version, locator, hash and NR status. Lidar's shared action roles and checked outcomes exclude raw command strings, paths and answers; feature importance is not treated as an isolated attack. SplitDecoy qualifies specifically through zero/nonzero returned-gradient support. Direct visual-token and gradient-value reconstruction remain adjacent under the auxiliary-only criterion. MIRAGE's privileged diagnostic is not recast as a low-permission privacy attack.

\subsection{What changes in the synthesis?}
For RQ1--2, the update adds tool-policy audits, structural campaign linkage, routing histories, tokenizer counts and distinct physical/sparsity/page-migration observers. These are different targets and permissions, not repeated demonstrations of each case. For RQ3, SplitDecoy supplies another outgoing path whose protection must be checked, while Kotevska et al.'s six-element claim register is close prior framework work; a tuple or generic composition warning is therefore not claimed as our novelty.

For RQ4, the positive defense evidence becomes stronger and more specific. CachePrune evaluates sensitive-token protection, QA/summarization quality and serving performance under selective sharing. Contextual reconstruction and detector false negatives retain their own endpoints. OTRO's optional epoch padding reduces additional access-count information from 3.94 to 0.04 bits conditional on byte length in a 20K-prompt model. Its TTFT text reports 4.59\% mean overhead for Llama-3.1, whereas the abstract rounds this to at most 4.5\%; the main TTFT figures explicitly omit padding. The padding cost is a separate calculation (up to about 2.8ms per epoch), not a measured end-to-end padded TTFT frontier. These details permit useful local claims without erasing residual byte-length information.

Jiao's packet-only and fused interrupt/packet endpoints have different permissions; SparLeak's separately measured attacker-monitoring slowdown is not the cost of its sparse-token defense. Shape and Substance's padding estimate is not a deployed paired intervention. These extraction differences change which cost can enter a deployment comparison. Consequently, the original 33-result inventory cannot support a collection-wide absence claim. Its comparisons remain reproducible for that subset; the added families supply new counterconditions and positive cases. The new controlled experiments separately test receiving and execution placement, without replacing old failed or negative observations.

The machine-readable merged index preserves every field of the original 60 rows and appends the 22 additions. The query log, per-record reasons and short evidence fragments, source hashes, correction ledger and sensitivity notes accompany it. Full third-party sources are identified by version/URL, not redistributed in the anonymous package.

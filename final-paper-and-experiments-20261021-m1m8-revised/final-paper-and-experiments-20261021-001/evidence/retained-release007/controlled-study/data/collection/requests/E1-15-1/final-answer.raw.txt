{"text":"Bridge inspections are regular checks that help engineers understand whether a bridge remains safe and usable. Inspectors look closely at parts such as the deck, railings, supports, and joints, watching for cracks, rust, loose connections, or damage from water and weather. They may walk the bridge, use binoculars or cameras, and reach hard-to-access areas with lifts, boats, or ropes. Some inspections also use instruments to measure movement, test materials, or examine hidden sections without causing significant damage. The findings are recorded so engineers can compare conditions over time, judge how serious any problem is, and decide whether repairs or closer checks are needed. Inspection schedules depend on the bridge’s design, age, location, and condition, and severe storms or collisions may prompt an extra visit; an inspection does not guarantee that every hidden flaw will be found, but it helps authorities manage risks and plan maintenance."}

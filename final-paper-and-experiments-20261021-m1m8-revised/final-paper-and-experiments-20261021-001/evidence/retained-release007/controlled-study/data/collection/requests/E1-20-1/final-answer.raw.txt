{"text":"An electric motor turns electrical energy into motion by using magnetic forces to make a shaft rotate. Inside a common motor, electric current flows through coils of wire, creating magnetic fields that interact with magnets or other energized coils. The interaction pushes and pulls on different parts of the motor, producing a turning force called torque. In many designs, a component called a commutator or electronic controller changes the current’s direction at the right moments, so the turning force continues instead of stopping or reversing. The spinning shaft can then drive wheels, fans, pumps, tools, or other machinery, while gears may adjust its speed and force to suit a particular job. Electric motors are useful because they can start quickly, operate precisely, and convert much of their input energy into motion, though some energy is still lost as heat, sound, and friction. Motors come in many sizes, from tiny devices in phones to large machines in trains and factories, and their design depends on the power supply and the motion required."}

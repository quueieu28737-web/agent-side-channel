{"text":"Urban trees are trees planted along streets, in parks, and around homes and businesses, where they share space with buildings and roads. Their leaves provide shade that can make sidewalks and buildings cooler, while their roots absorb some rainwater and help reduce runoff into drains. Trees also capture carbon dioxide as they grow, release oxygen, and trap some airborne particles, although the benefits depend on the tree species, its condition, and local surroundings. In addition, tree-lined places can support birds and insects, soften the appearance of built-up areas, and offer pleasant spaces for walking or resting. Growing trees in cities takes planning because roots need room, and branches must be kept clear of wires, signs, and traffic; dry soil, pollution, and heat can also make survival harder. Cities therefore choose suitable species, water young trees, inspect them for disease or damage, and maintain them over time so that their benefits can continue safely."}

{"text":"A heat pump is a device that moves heat from one place to another, rather than creating heat by burning fuel or using electric resistance. In winter, it draws warmth from outdoor air, the ground, or water and transfers that heat indoors, even when the outdoor temperature feels cold. In summer, many heat pumps can reverse direction, carrying heat from inside a building to the outdoors much like an air conditioner. Because moving heat generally takes less energy than making an equal amount of heat directly, heat pumps can use electricity efficiently and may reduce energy bills and emissions, depending on the local power supply and equipment. Their performance varies with outdoor conditions: air-source models can become less efficient in very cold weather, while ground-source systems use steadier underground temperatures but usually cost more to install. A heat pump works best when it is properly sized and installed, and when the building has good insulation and sealed drafts; some homes also use a backup heater during exceptionally cold periods."}

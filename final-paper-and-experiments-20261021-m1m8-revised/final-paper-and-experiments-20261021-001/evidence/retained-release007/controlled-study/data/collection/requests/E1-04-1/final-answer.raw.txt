{"text":"Water filtration is the process of removing unwanted particles and substances from water by passing it through a barrier or treatment material. A simple filter may trap visible dirt, sand, and rust, while finer filters can catch smaller particles that make water cloudy. Activated carbon is often used to reduce unpleasant tastes and odors and to capture some chemicals, but it does not remove every contaminant. Other methods, such as membranes, can remove very small particles and some dissolved substances, while disinfection with chlorine, ultraviolet light, or heat targets disease-causing microbes. No single filter works equally well for every problem, so the right choice depends on what is in the source water and what the filter is designed to remove. Filters also need proper installation and regular replacement or cleaning, since a clogged or poorly maintained device may work less effectively; testing water and following the manufacturer’s directions can help ensure the treated water is suitable for its intended use."}

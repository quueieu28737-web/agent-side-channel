{"text":"Wetland restoration is the work of helping damaged or drained wetlands regain their natural water patterns, plants, and ecological functions. A project may block drainage ditches, reshape land, or reconnect a wetland to a river so water can spread across the area at the right times. Restorers often plant native grasses, reeds, shrubs, or trees, while removing invasive species that crowd them out. These changes can create habitat and food for birds, fish, amphibians, insects, and other wildlife, and help wetlands filter pollution and trap sediment. Wetlands also store floodwater and can hold carbon in their waterlogged soils, although results depend on the site and how restoration is carried out. Recovery is usually gradual: workers and local communities may monitor water levels, plant growth, and wildlife for years, then adjust their approach if the wetland is not developing as expected."}

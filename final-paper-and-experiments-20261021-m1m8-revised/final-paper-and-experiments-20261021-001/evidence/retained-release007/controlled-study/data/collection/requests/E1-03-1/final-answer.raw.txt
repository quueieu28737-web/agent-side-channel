{"text":"Solar batteries store extra electricity produced by solar panels so it can be used later, such as at night or during cloudy weather. Most home systems use lithium-ion batteries, which charge when panels generate more power than the home needs and discharge when demand exceeds production. A battery can increase the amount of solar energy a household uses directly, reducing the electricity it buys from the grid, though savings depend on local rates and system costs. Some systems can also provide backup power during an outage, but only if they are designed to operate safely when the grid is down; the battery’s capacity determines how long selected appliances can run. Battery size is commonly described by its energy capacity, measured in kilowatt-hours, while its power rating indicates how much electricity it can deliver at one time. Batteries have upfront costs, lose some energy during charging and discharging, and gradually wear out, so choosing one involves balancing price, backup needs, energy use, and expected service life."}

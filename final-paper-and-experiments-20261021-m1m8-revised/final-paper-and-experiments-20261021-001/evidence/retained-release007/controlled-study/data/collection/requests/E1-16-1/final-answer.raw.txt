{"text":"Community gardens are shared plots of land where people grow vegetables, fruit, herbs, or flowers, often in a neighborhood with limited private yard space. Some gardens divide the land into individual plots, while others are tended collectively, with members sharing both the harvest and the work. They can provide fresh produce and a practical way to learn gardening skills, including how to prepare soil, water efficiently, and manage pests. Gardens also create places for neighbors to meet, exchange knowledge, and work together, which may strengthen local connections across generations and cultures. Organizing a garden takes planning: participants may need permission to use the site, access to water and tools, agreed rules, and a plan for upkeep when people are unavailable. Because land access, funding, soil quality, and volunteer time can be uncertain, gardens do not solve every food or neighborhood challenge, but they can offer a useful local resource when supported over time."}

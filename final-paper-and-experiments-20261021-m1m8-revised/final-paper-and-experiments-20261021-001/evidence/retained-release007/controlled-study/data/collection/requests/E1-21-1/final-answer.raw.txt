{"text":"Drip irrigation is a way of watering plants by delivering small amounts of water directly to the soil near their roots. A system usually carries water through a network of pipes or flexible tubing, with small outlets called emitters releasing it slowly along the route. Because water goes close to the plants rather than being sprayed over a broad area, less may be lost to wind, runoff, or evaporation from wet leaves. The method can be used in gardens, orchards, greenhouses, and farms, and it is especially useful where water is scarce or plants grow in rows. A filter is often included to keep dirt from clogging the narrow outlets, and a timer or valve can help control when and how long the system runs. Drip irrigation still needs regular inspection: leaks, blocked emitters, unsuitable pressure, or poorly placed tubing can leave some plants too wet and others too dry."}

{"text":"Bicycle gears change how hard you must pedal and how far the bicycle travels with each turn of the pedals. A low gear makes the pedals easier to turn, which is useful when climbing a steep hill or starting from a stop, but the wheels move a shorter distance per pedal turn. A high gear makes each pedal turn drive the bicycle farther, helping you travel quickly on level ground or downhill, though it takes more effort to get moving. On most geared bicycles, shifting moves the chain between differently sized sprockets on the rear wheel and, sometimes, the front crank. Moving the chain to a larger rear sprocket or smaller front chainring gives a lower gear, while a smaller rear sprocket or larger front chainring gives a higher one. You can usually shift while pedaling gently, but easing pressure on the pedals helps the chain move smoothly and avoids unnecessary wear; shifting before a hill becomes difficult can make riding more comfortable."}

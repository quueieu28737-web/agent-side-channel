{"text":"Acoustic insulation is a way of reducing the sound that travels from one space to another, such as between rooms or from outdoors into a home. It works mainly by adding barriers that block sound waves, sealing gaps where sound can leak through, and using materials that absorb some of the sound energy. Dense materials such as masonry or layered boards can make a wall harder for sound to vibrate through, while soft, porous materials such as mineral wool help limit sound inside cavities. The result depends on the whole construction, because even a small opening around a door, pipe, or window can weaken an otherwise substantial barrier. Acoustic insulation is different from sound absorption: insulation aims to reduce sound passing between spaces, whereas absorption helps control echoes and reverberation within a room. Good results usually come from combining suitable walls, floors, ceilings, doors, and seals, while also considering low-frequency sounds, which can be especially difficult to block."}

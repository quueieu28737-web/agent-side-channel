{"text":"Building ventilation is the process of replacing indoor air with outdoor air to help control odors, moisture, heat, and airborne pollutants. Some buildings rely on windows, vents, or leaks for this exchange, while others use fans and ducts to move air more predictably. In a mechanical system, fresh air may be filtered and warmed or cooled before it enters occupied rooms, and stale air is drawn out through separate outlets. Good design balances the amount of incoming and outgoing air, since too little ventilation can allow contaminants and humidity to build up, while excessive airflow can waste energy and make rooms uncomfortable. Ventilation is different from heating or cooling: those systems adjust temperature, whereas ventilation brings in outside air, although equipment may combine the functions. How much ventilation is needed depends on factors such as the building’s size, how many people use it, activities taking place inside, and local climate; filters, ducts, fans, and vents also need suitable upkeep to work well."}

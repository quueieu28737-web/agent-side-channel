{"text":"Composting is the natural process by which microorganisms, fungi, and small soil animals break down organic materials into a dark, crumbly substance called compost. A home compost pile can use fruit and vegetable scraps, coffee grounds, dry leaves, and untreated paper, while meat, dairy, and oily foods are generally best left out because they can attract pests or create odors. A useful mix combines nitrogen-rich “green” materials, such as fresh plant scraps, with carbon-rich “brown” materials, such as dry leaves or shredded cardboard. Keeping the pile slightly damp, like a wrung-out sponge, and turning it occasionally adds oxygen and helps decomposition proceed evenly. The process may take several weeks or many months, depending on the materials, pile size, temperature, and care; finished compost is usually dark and earthy-smelling, with few recognizable scraps. Gardeners spread it over soil or mix it into planting beds to add organic matter, improve soil structure and water retention, and support healthy plant growth, though compost is not a complete substitute for every fertilizer."}

{"text":"Public libraries are community institutions that provide free access to books, information, and other resources, usually funded by local or regional government. Their collections may include printed books, newspapers, films, music, audiobooks, and digital materials that people can borrow or use on site. Library staff help visitors find reliable information, use research tools, and develop skills such as reading, technology, and media literacy. Many libraries also offer public computers, internet access, quiet places to study, and welcoming spaces for people to meet. Programs such as story times, book groups, lectures, job-search workshops, and language classes make libraries useful beyond lending materials. Because libraries serve people with different ages, interests, and circumstances, they aim to make knowledge accessible without requiring users to buy books or subscribe to services. Borrowing rules, opening hours, and available programs vary by location, and some specialized resources may require a library card. In a changing information environment, libraries continue to adapt by expanding digital collections and teaching people how to evaluate what they find online."}

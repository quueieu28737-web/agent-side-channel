{"text":"A rain garden is a shallow, planted area designed to collect rainwater from roofs, driveways, or other hard surfaces. Instead of letting that water rush into a street drain, the garden holds it briefly while it soaks into the ground. Its soil and plant roots help filter some pollutants, such as sediment and excess nutrients, before water moves deeper into the soil. Rain gardens are often built with layers of amended soil and gravel, and they may include an inlet and a shallow overflow route for heavy storms. Plants are chosen to tolerate both wet periods and dry spells, so native grasses, flowers, and shrubs are common choices. A well-sited rain garden can reduce runoff and puddling, support beneficial insects, and add greenery to a yard, but it is not a pond and should drain within about a day or two; suitable placement, soil, and maintenance help it work safely and effectively."}

{"text":"Refrigeration slows the growth of many microbes by keeping food cold, which helps it stay safe and fresh for longer. Most household refrigerators should be set at 40°F (4°C) or below, while a freezer should be at 0°F (−18°C). Cold temperatures do not kill all bacteria, and some can still grow slowly, so refrigeration delays spoilage rather than preventing it entirely. Put perishable foods away promptly, ideally within two hours of purchase or cooking, and keep raw meat and seafood sealed on a lower shelf so their juices cannot drip onto other foods. Avoid packing the refrigerator so tightly that cold air cannot circulate, and use a refrigerator thermometer because the control dial alone may not show the actual temperature. Check stored food regularly, follow package dates and handling directions, and discard items that have been left warm too long or show clear signs of spoilage; when safety is uncertain, tasting is not a reliable test. Keeping the refrigerator clean and promptly wiping up spills also reduces the chance that microbes will spread between foods."}

{"text":"Wildfire prevention begins with reducing the chance that a spark will meet dry, easily ignited material, especially during hot, windy weather. Around homes, people can clear dead leaves and dry plants from roofs, gutters, decks, and the first several feet around buildings, while keeping firewood and fuel away from walls. In forests and grasslands, land managers may thin dense vegetation, remove accumulated brush, or use carefully planned prescribed burns to limit the amount of fuel available to a future fire. Anyone using machinery, vehicles, campfires, or outdoor equipment should follow local fire restrictions, maintain spark arresters, and make sure flames and hot ashes are completely out before leaving. Communities can also reduce harm by building with fire-resistant materials, maintaining accessible evacuation routes, and sharing alerts and preparedness plans with residents. Because lightning and accidents can still ignite fires, prevention is paired with early detection, prompt reporting of smoke, and coordinated response; people should contact emergency services rather than trying to fight a spreading wildfire themselves."}

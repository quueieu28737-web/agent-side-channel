{"text":"Glass recycling begins when people place bottles and jars in collection bins or take them to a recycling center. Workers or machines sort the material, removing items such as ceramics, mirrors, and drinking glasses, which have different compositions and can spoil a batch. The suitable glass is often separated by color, then cleaned to remove lids, labels, food, and other contaminants. It is crushed into small pieces called cullet, which can be melted in a furnace and formed into new bottles and jars. Using cullet generally requires less energy than making glass entirely from raw materials, because it melts at a lower temperature, and it reduces demand for materials such as sand, soda ash, and limestone. Glass can be recycled repeatedly without losing its basic quality, but the practical process depends on local collection rules, sorting equipment, and manufacturers’ needs, so residents should check what their community accepts and keep non-container glass out of the bin."}

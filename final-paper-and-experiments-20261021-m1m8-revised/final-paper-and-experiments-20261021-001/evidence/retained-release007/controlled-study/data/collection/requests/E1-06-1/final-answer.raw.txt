{"text":"Rail signaling is the system of signs, lights, and controls that helps trains travel safely along shared tracks. Signals tell drivers whether a section of track ahead is clear, occupied by another train, or reserved for a movement such as a crossing. Because trains are heavy and cannot stop quickly, signals provide warnings well before a train reaches a hazard or a place where it must slow down. Track circuits and other sensors can detect trains, while electronic systems send information between equipment beside the railway and the train itself. In many systems, a red signal means stop, while other colors or patterns indicate that a train may proceed, often with a speed limit or a warning to expect a stop ahead. Signaling also helps railway controllers coordinate routes at junctions, prevent conflicting movements, and keep trains spaced far enough apart, although the exact rules and equipment vary between countries and railway lines."}

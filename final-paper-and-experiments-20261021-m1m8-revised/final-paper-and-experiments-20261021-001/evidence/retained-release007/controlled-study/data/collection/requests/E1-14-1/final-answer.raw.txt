{"text":"Soil erosion is the gradual removal of the land’s upper layer, where many plants find nutrients and water. It happens naturally as rain, flowing water, wind, or ice loosens and carries soil from one place to another. Human activities can speed the process, especially when forests are cleared, fields are left bare, or construction disturbs the ground. Without roots and plant cover to hold it in place, exposed soil can wash into streams or blow away during dry, windy weather. Losing topsoil can make farms less productive, while sediment in rivers may cloud water, harm aquatic habitats, and contribute to flooding. Farmers and communities can reduce erosion by keeping soil covered with vegetation, planting along land contours, using terraces on slopes, and limiting unnecessary disturbance of the ground."}

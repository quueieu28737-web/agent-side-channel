\begin{abstract}
Encryption conceals content while exposing the structure of agent workflows. We systematize 82 study families through 39 source-bound result records, separating the observer, execution boundary and evaluation conditions that determine whether defense evidence transfers. The audit exposes three recurring mismatches: protection attached to the wrong receiving view, utility attached to a different state boundary, and cost attached to a different mechanism or attacker. Experiments on 690 real-agent runs turn these distinctions into measurable consequences. Customer-grouped cross-fitting shows approximately 30--35 percentage-point paired reductions in balanced accuracy under joint shaping in the standard-prompt arm, while a write-only ablation locates the dominant signal beyond a rules prompt's read-order constraints. The prompt supplies a behavioral control. Live tool measurements and stronger attacker comparisons expose timing leakage and substantial resource costs; the absolute security criterion remains unconfirmed. Classical traffic-shaping controls locate these results within a workload-specific cost comparison. The contribution is an auditable connection from published evidence to mechanism-level findings and deployment decisions.
\end{abstract}

Shadow state has three implementation paths. \textbf{(1) Authorized reads and verified no-ops} use the live backend with responses discarded; idempotence alone does not preserve state, and read cover cannot hide exposed write types. \textbf{(2) Provider-supported isolation} includes Kubernetes server dry-run, isolated Stripe sandboxes and transactional rollback \cite{kubernetesdryrun,stripesandboxes,postgresrollback}. Dry-run validates without persistence, sandboxes maintain separate state, and rollback covers transactional effects; sequence changes can survive rollback \cite{postgressequences}. \textbf{(3) A trusted MCP/tool gateway} decrypts authenticated cover markers and routes shadow writes to an isolated simulator. Real and cover traffic share an ingress envelope, with routing/timing/size differences treated as attack features. E13 measures a local gateway; the provider-specific paths are deployment designs.

Protection ends where an observer learns cover markers, routing branches or committed writes. The executor itself and provider commit logs are outside the ingress mechanism's protection (S17). Report leakage together with backend cost, latency, quotas and downstream observations. Cover consumes billable work and shared capacity and can amplify denial of service. Deployment requires per-tenant admission budgets, backpressure and separate sandbox credentials. Exhausted quotas must expose the loss of protection, rather than silently send shadow writes to live state.

\subsection{Ethics and artifact scope}\label{sec:ethics}
Executions use cloned public benchmark databases and loopback sockets; model requests contain public tasks and simulated interactions. No production tool or real account is modified. Stakeholders include paying tenants, providers sharing capacity and people whose records writes could affect. Isolation and bounded authorized cover are necessary for deployment. Other-account cancellation is a simulator control. No third-party live service was probed for a vulnerability, so no vendor-disclosure event is claimed. Released inference code has surveillance uses; the examples target public/synthetic identities and controlled local endpoints. Bot-detection evasion has a different objective and authorization boundary \cite{zhu2026turing,jeong2026wra}.

The artifact preserves source locators, model decisions, observations, frozen calibration and executable checks. AI assisted field extraction, implementation, source reconciliation and prose revision; authors verified the archived source-bound entries in \S2.3. This use addresses the volume of reconciliation and supplies no independent human ratings. Submission requires an anonymous immutable snapshot with identifying metadata removed; the development Git history is not that snapshot. Saved-output replays, model-generated trajectories and local TLS measurements are labeled separately.

\subsection{Threats to validity}
The purposive corpus has no field-wide recall estimate. Extraction--verification agreement is non-independent; standardized blind recoding sheets support future community audit. The 24 constructed cases measure rule application, not external decision accuracy. Eleven audit cells remain unverified after source inspection and three explicit status corrections.

E5 uses 48 saved outputs and a proxy tokenizer; E6/E7 use reference programs with known-population calibration. E9--E12 use one retail environment and a mutable model alias also serving as user simulator; development had access to all 115 tasks. Cross-fitting expands customer coverage within that population, and intervals condition on fitted attackers. E10--E14 are post hoc. Program conditions preserve reward structurally through trajectory reuse. E13 measures local RPC clocks on fixed actions under Windows scheduling; LLM generation, WAN transit, timing-dependent replanning and production quotas are unmeasured. A deployed tunnel horizon must also cover planner think time. The separate Luna extension supplies a same-provider retail comparison. New independent customers, airline/MCP transfer, fixed open weights, cross-vendor models and live replanning under cover remain untested. Power depends on independent positive customers and attacker correlation; repeating old tasks adds no independent customers.

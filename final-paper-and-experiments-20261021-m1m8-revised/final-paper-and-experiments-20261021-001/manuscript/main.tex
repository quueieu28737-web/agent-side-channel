\title{SoK: Evidence Boundaries of LLM and Agent Side-Channel Defenses\\A Result-Record Audit with Pre-Registered Transfer Tests}
\author{}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Published side-channel defenses protect specific observations and execution environments. Reusing these results in LLM-based workflows requires identifying which guarantees survive changes in receivers, tool state and attacker adaptation. We systematize \FinalN{} study families (\FinalCore{} core and \FinalAdjacent{} adjacent), retaining 39 scoped result records and auditing 20 mitigation families. A nine-item reporting audit and three deployment checks bind each result to its target, observer, execution boundary, workload and measured cost. Only 3 of 17 applicable families bind task or component utility, and 2 of 11 bind a matched adaptation contrast. To test whether the checks identify transfers that fail, we recorded seven audit judgments before running three controlled experiments. Five were confirmed and two partly confirmed; none was refuted. Streaming the same 48 saved responses as per-token TLS records shows that per-event padding leaves verbosity fully separable and identification at 58--62\% (chance 2.1\%). A buffered cap's 2.18$\times$ cost therefore does not price a stream. On 115 retail reference programs, randomized cancellation cover and decoy programs leave learned or count attacks at 77--100\% balanced accuracy, and clean-trained attackers read 50\% where refitted ones read 100\%. On an encrypted agent--tool link, request sizes identify the tool for 95.7\% of calls. Cover alone or framing alone leaves a channel open; only shape-equalizing sandboxed cover combined with constant framing approaches chance, at 26$\times$ bytes and 7.7$\times$ calls. These tests replay saved outputs and reference programs and do not evaluate an autonomous agent.
\end{abstract}

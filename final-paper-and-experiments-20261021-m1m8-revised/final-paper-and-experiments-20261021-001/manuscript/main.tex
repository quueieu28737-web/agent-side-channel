\title{SoK: Evidence Boundaries of LLM and Agent Side-Channel Defenses\\A Result-Record Audit and Two Controlled Case Studies}
\author{}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Published side-channel defenses protect specific observations and execution environments. Reusing these results in LLM-based workflows requires identifying which guarantees survive changes in receivers, tool state and attacker adaptation. We systematize \FinalN{} study families (\FinalCore{} core and \FinalAdjacent{} adjacent), retaining 39 scoped result records and auditing 20 mitigation families. A nine-item reporting audit and three deployment checks bind each result to its target, observer, execution boundary, workload and measured cost. The audit distinguishes established incompatibility from missing or unverified evidence; only 3 of 17 applicable families bind task or component utility, and 2 of 11 bind a matched adaptation contrast. Two controlled case studies illustrate the resulting evaluation obligations. Replays of 48 saved responses over two TLS~1.2 links reproduce downstream re-emission after upstream length padding. With matched workload denominators, 512-byte buckets cost 2.30$\times$ and 1.43$\times$ for two sentence-prefix workloads; a cap fitted to training lengths reaches 50\% test accuracy at 2.18$\times$ for the verbosity target. Replaying 115 retail reference programs shows that same-item writes can change workflow state, and that preserving task objects differs from preserving an account or a shared database. Simple count or position rules recover cancellation labels despite cover calls. These are local mechanism tests, not a combined autonomous-agent evaluation. The contribution is a reusable evidence audit that identifies which comparisons are supported and which require new measurements.
\end{abstract}

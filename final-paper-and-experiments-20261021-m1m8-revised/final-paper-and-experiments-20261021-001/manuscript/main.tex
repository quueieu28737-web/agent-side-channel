\title{SoK: Evaluating LLM and Agent Side-Channel Defenses\\From Evidence Audits to Real-Agent Measurements}
\author{}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Encryption hides message content while leaving the structure of agent workflows observable. Determining whether a side-channel defense protects a deployed workflow requires connecting the evidence across observation, execution and evaluation boundaries. We systematize \FinalN{} study families (\FinalCore{} core and \FinalAdjacent{} adjacent), with 39 scoped result records and an audit of 20 mitigation families. Our nine-item audit and three deployment checks identify concrete gaps: only 3 of 17 applicable families bind task or component utility, and 2 of 11 bind a matched adaptation contrast. Controlled experiments show why these bindings matter: per-event padding leaves streaming verbosity fully separable, refitting raises attack accuracy from 50\% to 100\%, and encrypted request sizes identify retail tools with 89.9--91.4\% held-out accuracy. We then evaluate 690 real GPT-6.1 Sol agent runs, calibrating the defense solely on 414 training runs before collecting 276 customer-disjoint test runs. In local TLS replay, online shadow cover with constant framing reduces cancellation/return network balanced accuracy from 97.2\%/94.0\% to 50.0\%/50.0\%, at 15.4$\times$ bytes and 3.8$\times$ calls. Workflow rules attain 97.1\% measured compliance while intent inference remains above 93\%. The intent signal lives in state-changing tool calls, which alone support 84--92\% accuracy; the rules standardize only the reads. Customer-grouped cross-fitting over all 690 runs confirms a 31--39-point reduction. It also identifies a bucketed operating point that reaches 52.2\%/52.7\% at 12.4$\times$ bytes, 37\% cheaper than constant framing at matching or lower leakage. The resulting evidence framework connects source-level audit findings to reproducible measurements of leakage, state preservation and deployment cost.
\end{abstract}

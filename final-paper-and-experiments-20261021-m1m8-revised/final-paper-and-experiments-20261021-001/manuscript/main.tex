\begin{abstract}
Encryption conceals message content while exposing the structure of agent workflows. We systematize \FinalN{} study families through a complete core-family map, 39 source-bound result records and a nine-item audit of 20 mitigation families. Three deployment checks connect this evidence to the observer's view, the effects of executed actions and matched attacker evaluation. They identify concrete reuse gaps: only 3 of 17 applicable families bind utility, and 2 of 11 bind a matched adaptation contrast. Controlled experiments make these gaps executable, showing how streaming counts, state-changing cover and defended-data fitting change protection claims. We then evaluate 690 real GPT-6.1 Sol agent runs with training-only defense calibration. Baseline network balanced accuracy (BA) reaches 97.2\%/94.0\% for cancellation/return intent. A rules prompt attains 97.1\% mechanical rule adherence while intent inference remains above 93\%. An observer ablation localizes the dominant signal to state-changing calls. Planner-transparent shadow cover with constant framing reduces baseline BA by 47.2/44.0 points to 50\% in the length/order view of local TLS replay. Customer-grouped cross-fitting retains large paired reductions and identifies a bucketed operating point at 52.2\%/52.7\% BA with 37\% fewer bytes than constant framing. A further 4,140 live tool sessions expose an 11.4-point timing-aware inference increase under serial cover and quantify the latency/resource trade-off against complete fixed-rate tunnels. The resulting systematization turns source evidence into concrete decisions about what to observe, where to intervene and how to evaluate protection and cost on the same workload.
\end{abstract}

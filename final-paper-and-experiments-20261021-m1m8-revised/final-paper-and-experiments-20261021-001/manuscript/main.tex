\begin{abstract}
Encryption conceals content while exposing the structure of agent workflows. We systematize 82 study families through 39 source-bound result records, separating the observer, execution boundary and evaluation conditions that determine whether defense evidence transfers. The synthesis identifies three consequential errors: attributing upstream protection to a plaintext-aware downstream emitter, treating discarded read cover as safe state-changing cover, and substituting one mechanism's utility or cost for another's. A populated family map and applicability-aware audit make these errors inspectable at their original source locations. Measurements on repeated agent trajectories from 53 customers test the resulting comparison obligations. Action-category localization, timing attacks and matched traffic-shaping controls expose both residual inference and costly protection boundaries; reductions in a fitted attack suite do not establish a security guarantee. The resulting contribution is a reusable evidence base and a set of demonstrated transfer failures that guide which published results can support a deployment decision, which comparisons require new measurements, and where inexpensive transport shaping matches more costly mechanisms on selected measured point metrics.
\end{abstract}

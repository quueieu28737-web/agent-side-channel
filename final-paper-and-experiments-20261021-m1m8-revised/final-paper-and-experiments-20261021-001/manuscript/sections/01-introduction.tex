\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}
Encryption conceals message content, yet LLM serving engines and agent systems expose packet sizes, cache timing, memory-access patterns, visited domains and tool-invocation sequences \cite{weiss2024prompt,promptpeek2025,earlybird2025,agentprint2025,uitraces2026,jeong2026wra}. A published defense result applies to a particular observer and operating point. Reusing it across a frontend, a tool dispatcher or another workload can change both the observable signal and the cost being measured. We turn this deployment problem into explicit, testable requirements for reusing defense evidence.

Our systematization connects published results to the receiving deployment through three operational checks:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Bind the receiving projection.} Target-oblivious postprocessing is a sufficient condition for preserving an upstream information bound (Remark~1, a direct consequence of the data processing inequality). A frontend with access to decoded content or an unprotected backward computation requires a separate argument or measurement \cite{netecho2025,politis2026splitdecoy}.
\item \textbf{Bind execution and protected state.} Read-only decoy requests and GUI actions operate on different interfaces and serve different objectives \cite{jeong2026wra,zhu2026turing}. For tools, unchanged argument values do not imply unchanged database state. The relevant contract must identify task objects, the user's account and shared state separately.
\item \textbf{Bind adaptation, utility and cost.} An attack on defended data must specify how the attacker was fitted; an overhead must identify the same workload and deployed mechanism as the claimed protection. Binding these quantities makes protection and cost comparable across deployments.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Contributions}
We connect a source-bound systematization to executable tests of defense reuse across observation and execution boundaries. The result is a measurement framework with three concrete outputs:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{A result-record repository:} \FinalN{} study families (\FinalCore{} core and \FinalAdjacent{} adjacent), with 39 scoped records and 78 utility/cost field positions from 26 families. The records distinguish source versions, receiver views, execution modes and cost objects that paper-level comparisons can conflate.
\item \textbf{An actionable comparability audit:} nine reporting items and three operational checks applied to 20 mitigation families. Utility is bound in 3/17 applicable families and a matched adaptation contrast in 2/11. The audit maps each unresolved reuse claim to the specific observation, state or evaluation evidence needed to resolve it.
\item \textbf{An executable bridge to real-agent defense evaluation:} controlled streaming and tool-state experiments culminate in 690 real-agent runs, with 414 training runs for defense calibration and 276 customer-disjoint test runs. Joint online cover and framing reduce cancellation/return network BA by 47.2/44.0 percentage points. A rules-prompt control reaches 97.1\% measured compliance while retaining over 93\% intent inference, separating behavioral compliance from metadata protection. Leakage localization explains why: state-changing calls alone support 84--92\% balanced accuracy, and the rules leave them unchanged. Cross-fitting over all 690 runs confirms the defense effect (31--39 points) and yields a bucketed operating point at 12.4$\times$ bytes, 37\% below constant framing.
\end{itemize}
The contribution is the connection between evidence and execution: result-level records expose ambiguous comparisons, operational checks specify the missing bindings, and controlled and real-agent measurements demonstrate their consequences. Composition, state isolation and adaptive evaluation provide the foundations for this reproducible assessment.

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}
Encryption conceals message content, yet LLM engines and agents expose packet sizes, cache timing, visited domains and tool sequences \cite{weiss2024prompt,promptpeek2025,earlybird2025,agentprint2025,uitraces2026,jeong2026wra}. A defense result applies to a particular receiver, execution boundary and workload. Reusing a headline score across a frontend, dispatcher or tool provider can change both its protected signal and its cost object.

Our SoK uses the \emph{evaluated result record} to make these transfers inspectable. Separating Dangol's stored-capture and live-protocol results prevents subtracting unmatched scores; separating Early Bird's KV-prefix and semantic-cache mechanisms prevents pricing one with the other's overhead. Combining signal origin with observer access and threat polarity explains why read-only privacy cover and state-changing bot evasion have different utility consequences. These are systematization findings: they emerge from binding evidence across records, rather than from proposing a new defense component.

\paragraph{Contributions}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{A populated evidence framework.} A complete map of \FinalCore{} core and \FinalAdjacent{} adjacent families, 39 source-bound records and a nine-item audit of 20 mitigation families. The framework identifies which comparisons are compatible, incompatible or unresolved.
\item \textbf{Three concrete failures of evidence transfer.} Frontend re-emission defeats an upstream-only protection claim; discarded retrieval cover does not justify live action cover; stored/live measurements and component costs cannot be interchanged. The record links show exactly which published comparisons fail and which positive examples bind the required evidence.
\item \textbf{Measured boundary checks on a common agent--tool chain.} Controlled streaming/state tests and repeated agent trajectories from 53 customers expose adaptation rebounds, state side effects and timing leakage. Post hoc customer-grouped analysis supplies descriptive comparisons, including cases where inexpensive transport adaptations match costly shadow shaping. Our own utility evidence is assessed under the same boundary requirements as the literature.
\end{itemize}
Shadow isolation, decoys, constant-rate tunnels and bucket padding have prior art. They are experimental probes of transfer claims here; we do not propose shadow shaping as a superior deployable defense. The comparison principles are established. The new synthesis is their application to source-bound records, including misleading within-paper comparisons that a paper-level summary would conceal.

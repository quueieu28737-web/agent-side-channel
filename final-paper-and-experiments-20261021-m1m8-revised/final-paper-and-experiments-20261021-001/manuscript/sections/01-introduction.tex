\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}
Encryption conceals message content, yet LLM serving engines and agent systems expose packet sizes, cache timing, memory-access patterns, visited domains and tool-invocation sequences \cite{weiss2024prompt,promptpeek2025,earlybird2025,agentprint2025,uitraces2026,jeong2026wra}. A published defense result applies to a particular observer and operating point. Reusing it across a frontend, a tool dispatcher or another workload can change both the observable signal and the cost being measured. We study this interpretation problem without assuming that the original authors claimed end-to-end protection.

Our systematization asks which evidence permits such reuse. It separates three obligations:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Bind the receiving projection.} Target-oblivious postprocessing is a sufficient condition for preserving an upstream information bound (Remark~1, a direct consequence of the data processing inequality). A frontend with access to decoded content or an unprotected backward computation requires a separate argument or measurement \cite{netecho2025,politis2026splitdecoy}.
\item \textbf{Bind execution and protected state.} Read-only decoy requests and GUI actions operate on different interfaces and serve different objectives \cite{jeong2026wra,zhu2026turing}. For tools, unchanged argument values do not imply unchanged database state. The relevant contract must identify task objects, the user's account and shared state separately.
\item \textbf{Bind adaptation, utility and cost.} An attack on defended data must specify how the attacker was fitted; an overhead must identify the same workload and deployed mechanism as the claimed protection. Missing bindings limit reuse without establishing that the original study omitted or failed the experiment.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Contributions}
We connect a source-bound systematization to executable tests of defense reuse across observation and execution boundaries. The result is a measurement framework with three concrete outputs:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{A result-record repository:} \FinalN{} study families (\FinalCore{} core and \FinalAdjacent{} adjacent), with 39 scoped records and 78 utility/cost field positions from 26 families. The records distinguish source versions, receiver views, execution modes and cost objects that paper-level comparisons can conflate.
\item \textbf{An actionable comparability audit:} nine reporting items and three operational checks applied to 20 mitigation families. Utility is bound in 3/17 applicable families and a matched adaptation contrast in 2/11. The audit maps each unresolved reuse claim to the specific observation, state or evaluation evidence needed to resolve it.
\item \textbf{A bridge from controlled mechanisms to real agents:} streaming TLS emitters, randomized cover on 115 retail reference programs, and a real-agent experiment with training-only defense calibration and a rules-prompt control. The current complete-task snapshot contains 414 training and 246 test runs. It quantifies how prompt compliance, execution isolation and transport shaping contribute different effects: rules leave intent inference above 92\%, while joint online cover/framing reduces both network point estimates to 50\% at a measured byte/call cost.
\end{itemize}
The framework builds on established composition, state-isolation and adaptive-evaluation principles. Its empirical contribution is their traceable application and cross-boundary measurement, rather than a new information-theoretic guarantee. Independent validation of the audit's decision accuracy remains an open question.

Table~\ref{tab:synthesis-decisions} links source-local findings to the new comparison judgments. For example, separating Dangol's stored-capture and live-protocol records prevents an invalid subtraction; separating Early Bird's two mechanisms prevents using a semantic-cache cost to price KV-prefix isolation. The transfer tests add consequences that a paper-level reading misses: a buffered padding cost does not price a stream, and leakage already present at a dispatcher persists through size-based decoding on the encrypted tool link.

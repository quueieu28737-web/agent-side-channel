\section{Implications, Ethics and Limits}\label{sec:agenda}
\subsection{Reporting obligations and open problems}
The nine audit items identify evidence needed for particular transfer claims: R1 target and protected interest; R2 receiver fields and linkage; R3 intervention placement and execution; R4 defended-condition fitting; R5 matched adaptation contrast; R6 workload and generator shifts; and R7--R9 inference, utility and cost. They do not require every component study to implement an autonomous agent. Inapplicable and unverified items must remain distinct from unreported evidence.

Three priorities follow. First, streaming defenses should report the record-sequence projection they equalize, together with completion and first-byte delay; per-event padding alone leaves counts (E5). Second, tool contracts should state the protected state scope and successful/rejected-call effects; same-value payloads and repeated-call idempotence differ from a no-change guarantee. Third, cover for agent tools should be evaluated against count rules, refitted learners and size-profiling decoders on every link that carries it. In our tests only sandboxed shape equalization with constant framing approached chance, and its 26$\times$ byte cost is itself an open problem (E7). Workload-level cache-reuse costs and cross-generator fingerprint portability remain separate open problems \cite{addagada2026kvgov,wu2026cacheprune}.

\subsection{Ethics and artifact scope}\label{sec:ethics}
The revision executes only local loopback sockets and cloned public benchmark databases. Sandboxed cover assumes a cooperating tool provider that executes designated calls without committing them; live cover is a simulator control. It invokes no production tool, sends no cover action to a real account and makes no new model API call. The synthetic and retail programs are prescribed reference executions; they are not observations of real user consent. The other-account cancellation condition is a simulator control, not a recommendation to cancel orders belonging to other users. A deployable decoy would require explicitly controlled resources and a separate isolation argument.

The corpus includes both confidentiality protection and bot-detection evasion. Authorization and protected interests must be stated: AHB's humanization objective differs from user privacy against a passive observer \cite{zhu2026turing,jeong2026wra}. Source analysis does not authorize evasion of third-party controls. The local artifact retains saved model outputs and their source provenance; model availability, terms at the historical generation date and fresh-generation reproducibility have not been independently revalidated in this revision. An accessible immutable anonymous repository remains a submission task; local files and hashes do not constitute a public release.

\subsection{Threats to validity}
The purposive corpus and narrow-query overlaps do not estimate field-wide recall. The four objective groupings overlap. Supplied coding columns support agreement calculations, while coder identity, blinding and independent adjudication cannot be established from those columns alone. Four added families were outside the baseline agreement sample. Cell-level historical bootstrap intervals and a high $\kappa$ do not establish external validity.

The 24 scenarios were constructed by the authors from the development corpus. Their labels and annotations are retained as illustrative material; no fair held-out superiority comparison is claimed. The synthetic indexing experiment has a negative joint result and supplies no proof of framework effectiveness.

TLS results condition on 48 saved outputs from one model, 12 held-out topics, prompted verbosity and two prefix constructions. Topic bootstrap intervals do not capture model, prompt or generation variability. Streaming uses a proxy tokenizer and application-layer padding rather than TLS~1.3 record padding; QUIC and timing attacks are untested, and latency is token-interval accounting. The retail experiments replay gold reference programs with three seeds, not autonomous planning; 18 cancellation and 32 return tasks make intervals wide. Cover parameters were fixed before execution and attackers were evaluated by task-grouped cross-validation, but the cover population is drawn from the same 115 programs. The size-profiling attacker assumes access to the public tool API. The predictions were written by the authors of the checks.

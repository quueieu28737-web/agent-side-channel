\section{Implications, Ethics and Limits}\label{sec:agenda}
\subsection{Reporting obligations and open problems}
The nine audit items specify what makes defense results comparable: R1 target and protected interest; R2 receiver fields and linkage; R3 intervention placement and execution; R4 defended-condition fitting; R5 matched adaptation; R6 workload and generator shifts; and R7--R9 inference, utility and cost. A component study can satisfy its own scope without implementing a complete autonomous agent; reuse across components requires the missing bindings.

Our measurements motivate three priorities. Streaming defenses should report the record sequence they equalize together with byte, completion-time and first-byte costs. Tool contracts should specify protected state and the effects of successful and rejected calls. Agent defenses should fit parameters on training workloads, test adaptive attackers on every exposed link, and measure prompt behavior separately from transport shaping. E9 demonstrates why: workflow rules achieve high measured compliance while metadata remains informative. Workload-level cache-reuse costs and cross-generator fingerprint portability remain separate problems \cite{addagada2026kvgov,wu2026cacheprune}.

\subsection{Ethics and artifact scope}\label{sec:ethics}
Tool executions and traffic captures use cloned public benchmark databases and local loopback sockets. E9 sends public benchmark instructions and simulated interactions to the model service; it modifies no production tool or real account. Shadow cover assumes a cooperating provider that does not commit its effects. Other-account cancellation is a simulator control, not a deployable decoy without controlled resources and an isolation argument. The corpus also covers bot-detection evasion; its authorization and objective differ from confidentiality protection \cite{zhu2026turing,jeong2026wra}.

The artifact preserves sources, raw observations, structured model decisions, frozen calibration and executable checks. AI-assisted implementation and manuscript revision do not constitute independent validation. The paper distinguishes saved-output replays, real model-generated trajectories and local TLS measurement.

\subsection{Threats to validity}
The purposive corpus and query overlaps do not estimate field-wide recall. Supplied coding columns support agreement calculations, but coder independence and adjudication are not established from those columns. The 24 author-constructed scenarios illustrate audit rules; external decision accuracy, incremental value over a simple checklist, and independent screening of the 617-record second search remain open.

E5 uses 48 saved outputs and a proxy tokenizer; E6/E7 use reference programs with known-population calibration. E9 completes all 690 planned runs with training-only calibration and a customer-disjoint test, removing the interim completion filter. Its scope is one public retail environment and one mutable model alias, also used for user simulation. Earlier development had access to all 115 tasks. The 46 test tasks include five positive cancellation customers and seven positive return customers; intervals condition on fitted attackers. Program conditions reuse trajectories, so equal reward is structural. External replication, broader model/environment transfer, provider-traffic measurement and interactive latency/feedback effects are the next evaluation targets.

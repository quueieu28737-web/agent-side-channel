A passive agent--tool observer can be an access-network operator, an authorized VPC flow monitor, or a relay on an inter-organization path. Shadow state has three possible implementations. \textbf{(1) Authorized reads and verified no-ops} use the backend with discarded responses; idempotence alone does not preserve state. \textbf{(2) Provider-supported isolation} includes Kubernetes server dry-run, separate Stripe sandboxes and transactional rollback \cite{kubernetesdryrun,stripesandboxes,postgresrollback}. These have different semantics: for example, sequence changes can survive rollback \cite{postgressequences}. \textbf{(3) A trusted tool gateway} authenticates cover markers and routes shadow writes to an isolated executor. Only this local gateway design is measured; the provider-specific paths are feasibility examples, not backend validations.

The trusted gateway/executor can identify cover. Protection ends at exposed markers, routing branches or commit logs; it does not protect against that principal. On the ingress link, the inexpensive WF adaptations are relevant competitors. At a restricted dispatch view, hiding call profiles additionally requires marker/routing isolation. Thus our results do not justify application-layer dummy execution for every passive-link deployment. Cover also consumes quotas and shared capacity; admission budgets, backpressure and separate credentials must prevent overload or accidental live writes.

\subsection{Ethics and artifact scope}\label{sec:ethics}
Executions use cloned public benchmark databases and loopback sockets; model requests contain public tasks and simulated interactions. No production account is modified or third-party service probed for a vulnerability. Stakeholders include paying tenants, shared-capacity providers and people affected by state changes. Other-account cancellation is a simulator control. Released inference code can support surveillance; examples use public/synthetic identities and controlled endpoints. Bot-detection evasion has a different authorization boundary \cite{zhu2026turing,jeong2026wra}.

AI assisted candidate-field extraction, implementation, source reconciliation and prose revision. This supports a large, source-linked comparison; it supplies no independent human judgments. Authors' source verification is specified in \S2.3. Saved-output replay, model-generated trajectories and measured local TLS are distinguished throughout. The artifact archives source locators, decisions, observations, calibration and executable checks. An anonymous submission snapshot must omit identity metadata and development history; the separate submission checklist records the AI-use disclosure and author review required at registration.

\subsection{Threats to validity and our own evidence audit}
The purposive corpus has no field-wide recall estimate. Archived extraction--verification agreement is non-independent; blind recoding sheets support future audit. Constructed rule cases and mechanism checks assess implementation consistency, not independent decision accuracy. Applicability-sensitive denominators and unresolved source cells are reported separately (\S6; S23).

Applying the same utility requirement to our experiments gives a precise boundary: undefended agent reward is measured; defended logical response/state conformance is checked; \emph{live planner utility under defense is not established}. E9/E10/E15 inherit reward from fixed trajectories, and E13 measures local RPC clocks under Windows scheduling, excluding LLM generation, WAN transit, deadlines and replanning. Neither state equality nor planner-message equivalence fills that gap.

E6/E7 calibrate on known reference programs. E9 freezes calibration before held-out collection, but development could access all 115 tasks. E10--E14 and the new sensitivity analyses are exploratory known-cohort analyses, not new-customer confirmation. Their intervals condition on fitted attackers and omit retraining uncertainty. E9's held-out set has only 5/7 positive customers; the full cohort has 14/18. Repeated runs increase execution coverage, not independent customer support.

Sol and Luna are mutable aliases from one provider in one retail environment, each also used in a separate user-simulator context. The Luna comparison has only 29 training episodes per prompt. Fixed open weights, cross-vendor transfer, a second environment, independent human recoding and live replanning under cover require new data. The SoK's source-bound comparisons and measured transfer failures stand separately from these untested generalizations.

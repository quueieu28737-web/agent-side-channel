\subsection{E13: live timing, latency and a complete tunnel}\label{sec:live-timing}
E13 executes all 690 saved agent action trajectories through a live local tool server under six conditions, yielding 4,140 new TLS sessions. Each real response gates the next action; the server executes real and shadow calls on separate states. The original customer-disjoint 414/276 training/test trajectory split is retained. Attackers are fitted and frozen on training captures before test collection. The relay records ciphertext lengths, directions and arrival times. These clocks measure the tool RPC chain, excluding LLM generation and WAN transit (S19).

The complete tunnel sends 4,096-byte cells in both directions at 2 or 5\,ms target intervals, including dummy cells. Its training-sized 133-slot horizon remains fixed after the last response; dummy cells execute no tools. Serial and two-in-flight shadow conditions use E9's prefix-only count policy and constant per-call framing. The full attack suite unions size/order, timing and joint features, plus learned flush removal for shadow cover; its detectors and transformations use training customers only.

\paragraph{Measure the exposed clock}
Under the original E13 suite on standard-prompt test trajectories, serial shadow cover yields 50.0/50.7\% size/order BA and 61.4/53.4\% under its full suite. In that original suite, the 2\,ms tunnel yields 62.5/52.9\% full-suite BA and the 5\,ms tunnel 53.1/57.3\%, with one tool call per original action at 125.7$\times$/125.7$\times$ bytes. Serial versus two-in-flight shadow scheduling has real-chain p95 latency of 26.2/20.8\,ms; the fast/slow tunnels have 56.4/112.3\,ms. \subsection{E14: stronger D2 attacks and classical controls}\label{sec:r7-main}
We fit seven R7 D2 models, including a trained 1D-CNN, k-FP-style leaf-fingerprint nearest neighbors and tabular learners, while retaining every original E13 attack. All fit on defended training customers under the public algorithm/configuration. The expanded maximum increases some tunnel scores; it preserves the serial-shadow clock result. Every bootstrap draw reselects the full maximum.

Table~\ref{tab:r7-controls} also adapts Tamaraw, FRONT, WTF-PAD and Walkie-Talkie to the same undefended captures. These are offline RPC adaptations: fixed cells preserve input-derived fragment sizes, directions and order, and dummy cells execute no tools. Random-donor WT reaches 50.0/56.7\% BA at 2.04$\times$ bytes; fast Tamaraw reaches 57.8/51.8\% at 11.99$\times$. Neither supports an absolute-security claim. WT lacks symmetric sensitive/non-sensitive pairing; the WTF-PAD adapter uses training-IAT histograms rather than the original Tor configuration. The short-customer FRONT calibration tests distribution mismatch without dropping test customers. S20 provides both prompts, algorithms and a cost plot under the same seven common attacks, alongside the larger available unions. Byte ratios share a denominator; measured RPC latency and simulated schedule span have distinct origins. These points expose cost/leakage trade-offs, not uniform shadow-cover superiority.

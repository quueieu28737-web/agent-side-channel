\subsection{E13: live timing, latency and a complete tunnel}\label{sec:live-timing}
E13 executes all 690 saved action trajectories through a live local tool server under six conditions: 4,140 new TLS sessions. Each real response gates the next action; real and shadow calls use separate states. The original customer-disjoint 414/276 training/test split is retained. Attackers are fitted and frozen on training captures before test collection. Ciphertext lengths, directions and arrival times measure the tool RPC chain, excluding LLM generation and WAN transit (S19).

The tunnel sends 4,096-byte cells in both directions at 2 or 5\,ms target intervals, including dummy cells that execute no tools. Its training-sized 133-slot horizon remains fixed after the last response. Serial and two-in-flight shadow conditions use E9's prefix-only count policy and constant per-call framing. The attack suite includes size/order, timing, joint features and learned flush removal; all transformations use training customers only.

\paragraph{The clock and the distinguishable cover}
For standard-prompt serial shadow cover, size/order BA is 50.0/50.7\%, versus 61.4/53.4\% with the original full suite. Cancellation increases by 11.4 points (paired 95\% interval [3.7,19.6]). Flush-phase detection reaches 90.4/92.3\% BA for serial/parallel schedules across 15,278 records from 276 test episodes (24 customers, both prompts). The distinct cover-versus-real request classifier reaches 82.5/96.1\% on 7,639 requests. These results reject cover indistinguishability in this implementation.

Detecting the terminal flush is not identical to recovering the task category. A training-fitted detector cuts the predicted suffix before intent classification; Table~\ref{tab:r8-flush} reports that composed attack. Its strongest return point is 60.7\% under rules. The overall attack union retains both stripped and unstripped traces, so removing a suffix never replaces a stronger attack. Scheduling leakage is a concrete failure mode, not an unmeasured caveat.

\paragraph{Price the protected boundary}
Serial/two-in-flight shadow schedules have real-chain p95 latency of 26.2/20.8\,ms, versus 56.4/112.3\,ms for the 2/5\,ms tunnels. Every session preserves real responses and final live state, checking the designed logical invariant. This does not measure defended planner utility. Fixed tunnel cell sizes/counts still expose host-scheduled direction/order; no measured condition meets the two-target absolute criterion. Transport dummies protect the passive link without executing tools. A trusted gateway is not a reason to prefer expensive shadow execution on that link; dispatch protection addresses a different, restricted projection that excludes the executor's routing knowledge.

\subsection{E14: matched attacks and classical controls}\label{sec:r7-main}
Seven added defense-aware models include a trained 1D-CNN, k-FP-style forest-leaf neighbors and tabular learners. Table~\ref{tab:r7-controls} separates a common seven-model comparison from the strongest available union retaining every E13 attack. All fit on defended training customers; each bootstrap draw reselects the suite maximum.

Under the common suite, random-donor Walkie-Talkie (WT) gives 50.0/56.7\% BA at 2.04$\times$ bytes, versus serial shadow's 50.0/52.9\% at 15.11$\times$: a trade-off, with no demonstrated network-efficiency advantage for shadow cover. WT also matches or improves both BA points, bytes and calls against parallel shadow. Under rules, WT gives 51.4/56.3\% at 2.23$\times$, improving those four point metrics over both shadow schedules (S23). These are descriptive point comparisons, not statistical dominance or equal-utility comparisons.

The WF rows are offline RPC adaptations, not reproductions of original website-fingerprinting guarantees. WT lacks symmetric site pairing; WTF-PAD uses training-IAT histograms rather than its Tor configuration. Input-derived fragments preserve sizes, directions and order; this is not a decryption or live-planner utility check. Offline schedule spans and measured RPC latency have different origins. S20 supplies all algorithms, both prompts and common-suite intervals. The evidence favors cheaper transport alternatives on some measured points and supplies no minimum-cost or absolute-security claim.

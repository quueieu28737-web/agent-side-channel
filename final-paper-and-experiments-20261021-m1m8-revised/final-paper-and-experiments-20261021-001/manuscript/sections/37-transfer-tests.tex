\section{Controlled Transfer Tests}\label{sec:controlled-placement}\label{sec:tau-cover-bridge}
The experiments connect observation, execution and matched evaluation. We retain artifact identifiers: E1/E3 cover buffered transport and adaptive fitting (\S8.1; S11), E2 tests state-preserving cover (\S8.2; S9/S11), E5--E7 isolate streaming and dispatch mechanisms, E8 tests stand-in planners (S13), and E9--E12 analyze real-agent trajectories. S18 gives the full concordance, including unused E4. Seven author-constructed mechanism checks were recorded before E5--E7 (S12); they are engineering checks rather than a sample of external review decisions.

For E6/E7/E9, a cell meets the \emph{evaluated-suite criterion} when maximum BA has an upper 95\% bound below 0.60 and maximum AUROC is below 0.65. This absolute-threshold assessment is separate from the measured reduction relative to raw transport: a lower BA bound above 0.60 indicates above-threshold leakage, while an interval crossing 0.60 leaves the absolute threshold inconclusive. We recompute the suite maximum within each of 2,000 common bootstrap draws: tasks for E6/E7 and customers for E9, retaining their tasks and repeats together. Intervals condition on fitted predictions and do not cover unknown attackers.

A byte-transparent relay captures post-handshake TLS application records on loopback connections. TLS~1.3 session tickets are disabled. E5--E8 use saved text or stand-in planners; E9 invokes a real model and executes its structured actions in the local public retail simulator. No production account or tool is modified.

\subsection{Transport: buffered and streaming emitters}
\paragraph{Buffered emitter}
We use brief and expanded \texttt{gpt-6-luna} responses for 24 topics (12 training, 12 test). In a two-link TLS~1.2 replay, decoding and re-chunking leave the terminal link 100\% separable when only the upstream link is padded; padding both reduces the classifier to 50\%, reproducing NetEcho's mechanism \cite{netecho2025}. A 1,280-byte cap fitted on training outputs yields 50\% verbosity inference at 2.18$\times$ bytes. Across offsets, buckets and random tails, defended-data fitting gains 13.5--50.0 points over clean fitting; constant caps give no gain. Supplement S11 records matched costs and all cells.

\paragraph{E5: the same texts as a token stream}
E5 emits these 48 texts as per-token server-sent events over TLS~1.2/1.3, reflecting the streaming surface attacked in prior work \cite{weiss2024prompt,whisperleak2025}. Tokenization uses o200k\_base; latency is measured in token intervals. We compare random per-event padding, constant events, padded eight-token batches and constant events with dummy events to the largest training count. Attackers include clean- and defended-trained classifiers plus a closed-world profiler trained on an independent emission of all 48 texts.

Table~\ref{tab:transfer-streaming} resolves P1--P3. Per-event padding preserves token count: verbosity remains 100\% separable and identification reaches 58--62\% (chance 2.1\%). Batching cuts bytes to 0.21$\times$ and clean-trained BA to 50\%, but refitting recovers 100\%. Constant shape reaches 50\% at 2.42$\times$ bytes and 208 versus 105 token intervals; two overflowing test outputs remain identifiable. A buffered cap's first byte arrives after generation, so its cost cannot price this streaming protection. E5 binds the protection claim to its emitter and cost object.

\subsection{Execution: state scope and randomized cover}
\paragraph{State scope}
In the 115 public retail programs, same-item modification changes status and appends a refund even when item and price are unchanged (173/291 pre-insertion units). State preservation $f(s,a)=s$ therefore differs from idempotence $f(f(s,a),a)=f(s,a)$. Cancelling another user's order changes the shared database. Shadow cover separates an observable call from its live effects: authorized reads, noncommitting API operations or a trusted gateway's isolated executor can supply this boundary (\S9.1). A known-position cover call remains removable with 100\% accuracy (S11).

\paragraph{E6: randomized cover against policy-aware attackers}
E6's dispatch observer sees ordered tool names and response lengths. Cover is sampled from other users' programs, randomly interleaved and executed on shadow state, with live execution as a control. Policies add cancellation, pad its count, interleave decoy programs or equalize every tool's count. Templates, donors and caps use all 115 reference programs: this is known-population defense calibration. Attackers use clean/defended gradient boosting and logistic regression on counts, bigrams and lengths, plus a count rule. Task-grouped 5-fold evaluation uses three seeds and cancellation/return targets (18/32 positive tasks).

Table~\ref{tab:transfer-cover} shows that one cancellation drives clean-trained BA to 50\%, but refitting recovers 100\% (P2). Count equalization defeats the count rule while correlated tools yield 76.9\% (P4). Decoy programs, the read-only pattern of WRA \cite{jeong2026wra}, leave 83.5--91.9\%. Shape equalization reaches 50.0\%/54.5\% at 6.7 extra calls per real call. Database equality is 100\% for shadow execution and 0\% for live cancellation, demonstrating the importance of the execution boundary.

\subsection{E7: composing execution and transport on the agent--tool link}
E7 composes dispatch and transport on an encrypted agent--tool link (Figure~\ref{fig:system-model}). Four cover policies yield 4,140 TLS~1.2 sessions. Framing is raw, 1024-byte buckets or constant requests/responses with overflow buckets. The evaluated network projection contains direction-tagged TLS record lengths and order; it excludes tool names and inter-arrival times. A profiling attacker learns size-to-tool mappings from training-fold API calls and applies dispatch attacks to decoded traces; S12 specifies caps and classifiers.

Request-only tool identification reaches 89.9\%/91.4\% under cancellation/return folds (582 calls; majority 29.4\%). Count and decoy cover leave 72.7--95.7\% network BA, extending their dispatch leakage (P5). Framing alone leaves bucketed return BA at 73.9\%. Shape cover gives 51.2\%/60.5\% with raw framing and 54.4\%/50.0\% with constant framing. No configuration meets the absolute criterion for both targets. Joint constant framing costs 26.3$\times$ bytes and 7.7$\times$ calls with 100\% database equality: one operating point, not a minimum cost (P7). E8's stand-in planners (S13) motivate the causal, training-only dispatcher evaluated next with real model decisions.

\paragraph{Transport link versus dispatch boundary}
Per-call framing leaves call counts and turn structure exposed. A full traffic-flow-confidentiality tunnel can instead conceal these from a passive link observer using fixed-size, constant-rate traffic \cite{rfc9347}; protecting session duration additionally requires a workload-independent traffic horizon. Its dummy packets need not execute tools. E7/E9 test per-call framing, not such a tunnel. At a dispatch boundary that exposes calls after decapsulation, transport padding is removed and call-level cover becomes relevant. Protection then depends on hiding cover markers and downstream routing from that observer (S17).

\subsection{E9: real agents and training-only defense calibration}\label{sec:real-agent}
E9 tests defense reuse on autonomous model-generated trajectories under a strict calibration boundary. GPT-6.1 Sol (low reasoning effort) plans and responds from the retail policy, public tool schemas and actual sandbox observations. A separate context of the same model simulates the user. The rules arm adds one customer-details read before order access and a read before each order write. All 115 tasks have two prompt arms and three repeats, completing the frozen 690-run protocol. All 414 training runs on 69 tasks precede freezing of a shared count template, donor pools and framing caps; the 276 test runs cover 46 tasks from 24 disjoint customers.

\paragraph{Planner-visible equivalence}
Let $H$ be the planner's message/tool-observation history, excluding wall-clock timing. Real calls and responses pass unchanged; cover uses isolated state and hidden responses. Thus the planner projection satisfies $\pi_{\rm plan}(D(H))=H$ (Appendix~\ref{app:equivalence}). E9 replays each realized trajectory through four program conditions, isolating the program-layer effect from LLM sampling variation. Online denotes prefix-only scheduling; this equivalence does not preserve elapsed time, queueing or timeout behavior. The trusted cover executor knows which calls are shadow calls; E9 protects the measured dispatch/network projections, not this executor or a provider's commit log.

The dispatcher inserts training-derived cover using only past calls and flushes unmet counts at episode end. A timing-aware observer could exploit this flush or read/write service times; E9 measures the length/order projection, not a defense against that observer. Four program conditions per prompt isolate raw transport, padding, shape cover and their combination. Training-fitted attackers evaluate dispatch and network observations across 2,760 TLS replay sessions. Every test task, failure and handoff remains in the analysis. Supplement S14 supplies the full ablations and customer-cluster intervals.

\paragraph{Rule-adherent workflows still reveal intent}
Table~\ref{tab:r4-primary} shows baseline network BA of 97.2\% for cancellation and 94.0\% for return. Rules raise mechanical adherence to the two read-order rules from 86.2\% to 97.1\%, yet network BA remains 97.2\%/93.5\%. Task reward is 110/138 (79.7\%) for baseline and 105/138 (76.1\%) for rules; the paired difference is $-3.6$ points (95\% interval $[-9.5,1.4]$). Thus workflow rules successfully alter mechanical rule adherence while leaving a strong intent signal in the encrypted trace.

\paragraph{Joint shaping removes most measured inference advantage}
On baseline trajectories, shadow cover with constant framing reduces network BA by 47.2/44.0 points to 50.0\%/50.0\% at 15.4$\times$ bytes and 3.8$\times$ calls. Per-call padding alone leaves 66.1\%/55.5\%, and shape cover alone 60.8\%/51.9\%. These ablations motivate jointly shaping length and call structure at the measured boundary; they do not establish superiority over a complete tunnel.

The joint point estimates match the 50\% majority baseline, removing the measured BA advantage of this attack suite. Post hoc paired customer-bootstrap contrasts quantify the reduction: $-47.2$ points [$-50.0,-33.3$] for cancellation and $-44.0$ [$-48.9,-27.8$] for return (Table~\ref{tab:r4-primary}). The separate absolute-threshold assessment remains inconclusive: joint BA upper bounds are 60.6\%/65.5\%. Only five cancellation-positive and seven return-positive customers contribute to these estimates; resampling their heterogeneous predictions and reselecting the suite maximum can exceed 50\% even when the original maximum equals 50\%. These finite-cluster intervals do not certify the absolute bound; their width cannot be attributed solely to sample count. The paired reductions answer a separate question. Rules plus joint shaping yields 50.0\%/60.6\%; its return contrast with program-only spans zero (S14).

\subsection{E10--E12: cross-fitting, operating points and mechanism}\label{sec:r5}
\paragraph{E10: cross-fitting}
A post hoc customer-grouped stratified 5-fold analysis reuses all 690 runs: 53 customers, including 14 cancellation-positive and 18 return-positive customers. Each fold fits defense templates, donors, caps and attackers on the other customers only. Its analytic TLS~1.2 model matches all 2,760 measured sessions and 120 fresh captures; the implementation also reproduces the original frozen-split results. Table~\ref{tab:r5} shows 91.7\%/85.5\% undefended BA. Recalibrating the E9 constant-framing design reduces BA by 31.2/33.4 points (paired intervals $[-41.6,-21.5]$/$[-41.0,-21.5]$). Rules-minus-baseline intervals under bucketed shaping span zero. An extended attacker suite changes these two operating points by at most 1.7 points (S15).

\paragraph{E11: choosing the operating point}
Among six evaluated configurations, 1024-byte buckets reach 52.2\%/52.7\% BA at 12.4$\times$ bytes versus 19.8$\times$ for constant framing; the upper bounds are 60.5\%/60.2\%. Selecting the cheapest configuration with all four inner-fold BAs $\leq60\%$, using only the original 69 training tasks, picks buckets. On the 46-task test split, cost falls from 15.4$\times$ to 9.8$\times$ bytes at 53.2\%/58.4\% BA. This retrospective analysis neither changes the E9 threshold nor supplies an independent confirmatory test; neither baseline operating point certifies the two-target absolute bound.

\paragraph{E12: locating the signal}
An oracle ablation retains 91.7\%/84.9\% BA using only state-changing calls; removing them leaves 55.2\%/59.9\%. Rules reduce distinct read sequences from 74 to 62, while write sequences remain 37 versus 36. This localizes the dominant measured signal beyond the reads targeted by the prompt. The tested writes-only defenses fail the inner-fold selection rule; full-profile shaping supplies the selected operating point.

\subsection{What the cases establish}
Each experiment instantiates one audit obligation and changes a reuse judgment. E5 shows that the emitter fixes both the leaking count and the cost object. E6/E7 show that refitted attackers and encrypted size decoding recover tool intent that clean-trained or dispatch-only evaluations miss. E9--E12 carry these bindings to real agents: leakage concentrates in state-changing calls, behavioral compliance does not reach it, and training-only program shaping removes most of it at a measured, tunable cost.

\soktakeaway{SoK Takeaway 3 (Bind the projection the emitter produces)}{Padding applies to the record sequence it equalizes. Buffered caps, per-event padding and batching have distinct leakage and cost objects. Evaluate the attacker on defended data and price the same emitter that supplies the protection claim.}

\soktakeaway{SoK Takeaway 4 (Shape what the agent changes, and price it)}{Across 690 real-agent runs, rules yield 97.1\% mechanical rule adherence while intent inference remains above 93\%. The observer ablation localizes the dominant signal to state-changing calls. Training-calibrated joint shaping lowers inference by 31--47 points; bucketed framing reaches 52--53\% at 12.4$\times$ bytes, 37\% below constant framing. Evaluate behavioral compliance, state preservation, metadata protection and cost as distinct outcomes.}

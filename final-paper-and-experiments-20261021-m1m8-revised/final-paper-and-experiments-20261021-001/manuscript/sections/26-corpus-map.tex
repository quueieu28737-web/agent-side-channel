\paragraph{Cross-origin synthesis}
Origin B includes token/packet lengths, cache timing and co-resident hardware channels; Origin A includes visited domains, tool dispatch and browser actions. Receiver permissions cut across these origins: a remote network observer, a same-site script and a privileged diagnostic hook expose different views even when they share an attribution target. Thus Origin A/B cannot substitute for Types I--IV or observer permissions. WRA's read-only privacy decoys, AHB's stateful evasion and SplitDecoy's exposed return path illustrate transfers that fail when these axes are collapsed \cite{jeong2026wra,zhu2026turing,politis2026splitdecoy}. The map retains every core family rather than using these examples as a substitute for coverage.

\subsection{Relationship to prior systematizations}
Prior surveys establish compound-system threat models and confidential-computing boundaries. Banerjee et al.\ categorize cross-layer compound AI threats under MITRE tactics; Forough et al.\ distinguish TEE isolation boundaries from side-channel resistance and note that inference protections need not cover agent memory or action coordination \cite{banerjee2024compound,forough2026secrets}. Kotevska et al.\ propose a six-element privacy contract (asset, observer, channel, permitted disclosure, guarantee, evidence) with composition conditions framed as formal conjectures \cite{kotevska2026privacyfoundations}, while classical website-fingerprinting works establish bandwidth/latency bounds for transport shaping (Tamaraw, WTF-PAD, FRONT, Walkie-Talkie) \cite{cai2014systematic,juarez2016wtfpad,gong2020front,wang2017walkietalkie}. Our distinct contribution is the populated 82-family / 39-record repository, the nine-item comparability audit, and mechanism checks recorded before E5--E7 (S12) for the transfers it flags, using loopback TLS replays and a pinned stateful tool simulator. Website-fingerprinting defenses already show that per-packet padding leaves counts and that constant-shape shaping is expensive \cite{cai2014systematic,juarez2016wtfpad}. E5 measures how that known property invalidates a specific cost transfer for LLM token streams, without claiming it as new.

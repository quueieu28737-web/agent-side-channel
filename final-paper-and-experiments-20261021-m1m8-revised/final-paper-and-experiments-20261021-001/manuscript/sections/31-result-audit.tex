\section{Result-Pair Comparability, Nine-Item Split Audit and Dual-Coding}\label{app:result-audit}
This audit applies the paper's comparison rules to the 33 result records across 22 families (and the nine-item split reporting checklist to the 16 baseline and 4 newly added evaluated-mitigation families). The supplied second-coder columns are retained for agreement analysis; coder independence and blinding are not verified by these files (\path{experiments/revision-m1-m8/}).

\subsection{Within-family and within-cohort pairwise comparability}
While the machine-readable inventory retains all $\binom{33}{2}=528$ unordered pairs for completeness, 426 pairs cross unrelated mechanism cohorts and are outside scope by definition. Table~\ref{tab:pair-descriptive} summarizes two nested informative subsets (the 13 are included in the 102): the \textbf{13 within-family pairs} and the \textbf{102 within-cohort related pairs} (traffic-envelope: 66; cache-service: 28; behavioral humanization: 3; generator fingerprint: 3; local-memory: 1; fixed guardrail: 1).

Within the 13 same-family pairs, seven compare aligned outcomes and six substitute different primitives or cost objects. Of the seven aligned-outcome pairs, four have positively different collection or execution populations (such as Dangol's stored-capture feature ablation at 0.17 accuracy versus live loopback OHTTP at 0.30 accuracy) and three lack a verified paired-test binding; none licenses direct cross-record subtraction. Across the 50 dual-coded pairs (all 13 same-family pairs plus 37 stratified related-cohort pairs = 150 judgments across descriptive, paired-effect and transfer rules), recorded two-column agreement is \textbf{94.0\% (141/150)} with \textbf{Cohen's $\kappa=0.916$} (95\% bootstrap CI $[0.856,0.963]$; \path{m7_dual_coding_pairs.csv}).
